## Naming Constraints and Preferences

### UK Company-Name Rules

- The legal company name must be unique on the Companies House register and not "too like" an existing active company name.
- You can generally reuse a dissolved name, as long as it is not too similar to an active one and you were not involved in an insolvent liquidation of that company.
- The legal name for a limited company must include "Ltd" or "Limited".

### Trading Names vs Company Names

- You can have a compact, flexible legal name (e.g. "ArdoTech Ltd") and use more descriptive or wine-specific trading names without further legal registration.
- For something like the London Wine Fair visitor registration, you can simply use your chosen trading name plus your job title; no formal incorporation is required at that point.

### Preferences

- Able to pivot: wine is a strong current interest, but may want the brand to support wider AI/ML or scientific computing work.
- Something that hints at wine but is not locked into only wine, and not overly cute.
- The "Ardo" root (from Basque "ardo" = wine) and the "Tech"/"techa" play on technology.

## Name Directions Discussed

### VinoTech / Vino-type Names

- **Pros:** immediately legible as wine + tech.
- **Cons:** various existing and dissolved companies; some risk of confusion with existing brands; feels a bit generic.

### EnoTech / Enotec Variants

- **Pros:** rooted in "eno-" (wine), familiar to wine insiders.
- **Cons:** there are active UK companies with close names; greater risk of "too like" issues and confusion.

### ArdoTech and Variants

- **Pros:**
  - "Ardo" is a wine root but not obvious to everyone, so the name is not locked exclusively into wine.
  - Less crowded in UK wine-tech naming space, so more room to build your own meaning into it.
  - Fits well with identity as a scientist/technologist first, wine specialist second.
- **Cons:**
  - Slightly opaque to the average person; may need a clarifying tagline or descriptor.
